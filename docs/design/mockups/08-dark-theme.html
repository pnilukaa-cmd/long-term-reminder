Dark theme — full palette, status legibility, elevation (new)

Companion to docs/design/02-v1-design.md §7a. This is the designer-specified dark palette that replaces developer's placeholder tonal derivation — every role was retoned from its own hue/chroma anchor (not inverted), status legibility is verified to carry over unchanged, the Health check tint is resolved against the error role with a quantified fix, and two real "breaks in dark" bugs in the shared theme code are flagged with concrete corrections. All CSS custom-property names below match the light mockups' :root block exactly — same tokens, dark values.

The warning role needed a hue correction, not just a lightness inversion. A naive same-hue retone of light's warning (Lab hue ≈41°) up to a dark-appropriate lightness renders as salmon-pink — nearly on top of dark error's hue (≈31°). At high lightness, that hue angle reads as peach, not gold; amber needs to sit further round the wheel (≈83°) to still read as amber once it's light enough to work on a dark background. Fixed by rotating warning's dark anchor to hue 83° (#EBC070/#634700) — this is a genuine deviation from a literal "retone the same hue" derivation, called out explicitly per the handoff instructions. See §7a in the design doc for the full reasoning and the sweep that found it.

Health check vs. error — resolved for dark. The doc flagged this in light as "closest hue in the system to error, worth a colorblind check." In dark the usable range narrows (dark error is a fixed, saturated #93000A/#FFB4AB — there's less room to keep a warm pink comfortably clear of it), so I did the check rather than re-flag it.

What I measured: a literal same-hue retone of light's Health tint (hue 348°) into dark tone-30/90 would put it ~43° from dark error's hue (31°). I rotated it further, to hue 335° (still reads as "berry/plum," the family the doc's copy already promises) — ~56° separation, and checked it doesn't just trade one collision for another: at 335° it's still a comfortable ~26° from Professional Licence's violet (309°), the next-nearest category hue.

Then I actually ran a colorblind simulation rather than reasoning about hue angles alone — Machado et al. 2009 protanopia/deuteranopia matrices applied to both containers. Under simulated deuteranopia, error-container renders as olive-brown (#5C5100) and the chosen Health-container renders as blue-slate (#434A5F) — a ΔE of 56 in Lab space (ΔE >10 is normally considered clearly distinguishable; this is over 5× that). The naive same-hue version scored ΔE 46 — still probably fine, but the rotation adds real margin rather than a coat of paint. The reason it survives simulation at all despite being close in raw hue-angle: pink/magenta carries more blue than red-orange does, and dichromats retain blue-axis discrimination even when they lose red-green — so pushing further toward violet is exactly the right axis to move on, not arbitrary.

Belt-and-braces — form, not just hue: the two are never colliding UI elements even in principle. Health's badge uses its own heart+pulse glyph and sits in the card's left type-badge slot; Overdue's status dot uses a distinct pennant-notch silhouette (never reused for any category badge) and sits in the card's right status-row slot. Per §6's own rule, hue is the backup signal here, not the primary one — form and position already do most of the work.

4. Elevation in dark — tone, not shadow (a light-mode assumption that breaks here)

Material 3 dark mode conveys elevation with a tonal surface ladder — each higher surface uses a progressively lighter tone of the same near-black hue — because a drop shadow (always literally black) is nearly invisible against an already-dark background. The light theme's elevation cue leans on shadow plus a card that's whiter than the page (surfaceContainerLowest, tone 100, on a tone-98 scaffold). That specific pairing does not translate: in the shared _themeFrom() in app_theme.dart, cardTheme.color is unconditionally colorScheme.surfaceContainerLowest for both schemes — in dark that resolves to tone 4, darker than the tone-6 scaffold behind it. A card would render as a faint hole in the page, not an elevated surface. This is a confirmed defect in the shared derivation, not a hypothetical.

Second confirmed "breaks in dark" bug: the snackbar. snackBarTheme in app_theme.dart hardcodes backgroundColor: Color(0xFF2B2B33) for both schemes. In light that's a deliberate dark-neutral chip that pops against a light scaffold — it works precisely because it's the opposite brightness of the page. In dark, #2B2B33 sits almost exactly inside the surfaceContainerHigh/surfaceContainerHighest tone range (#2A2932–#35343D) — the toast would nearly disappear into the page it's supposed to float above. Fix: use M3's actual intended role for this component — inverseSurface/onInverseSurface, which is defined to always render as "the opposite scheme's surface," so it stays legible regardless of theme. Dark's inverseSurface computes to #E3E2E9 (a light neutral, i.e. what light-mode's card looks like) with onInverseSurface #303036 — a light toast on a dark screen, which is also standard platform behavior elsewhere on Android. See the toast state in the Item detail tabs below.
